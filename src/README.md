# Source

- `app.html` – the whole app (single file, no framework). It is also the Claude artifact version; blocks between `<!--CLAUDE-ONLY-->` and `<!--/CLAUDE-ONLY-->` are removed for GitHub Pages.
- `build.py` – builds the GitHub Pages version into the repo root: `python3 src/build.py` (or `python3 src/build.py /some/out/dir`). Adds the iPhone/PWA head, service worker, icons, gist sync between devices, and the feedback button. Needs Pillow.
- `icon.png` – app icon source (navy ą in Półtawski Nowy with a coral tick, on cream).
- **Theme “Kraków”** (colour tokens at the top of `app.html`, light and dark): granat navy for actions, coral (the korale necklace) for the red pen, grades and the round's progress beads, on cream; kept low in contrast on purpose. Headings and Polish sentences in Półtawski Nowy, everything else in Lato, marks in Caveat. Big filled surfaces use `--tile` / `--btn`, so dark mode can keep them muted.
- **Grammar layer** (top of the script in `app.html`, between the helpers and the content):
  - `LEX` – the word list: every word the app uses (plus vocabulary from the grammar modules, tagged `L:'later'`), with its type, gender (`m1` masculine personal, `m2` animate, `m3` inanimate, `f`, `n`; plural-only nouns `pl` + `vir`), verb pattern (`am` -am/-asz, `em` -em/-esz, `esz` -ę/-esz, `isz` -ę/-isz/-ysz, `irr`) and all its forms. `FORMS` indexes every form with its tags (case, number, person, gender). Adjective endings are generated (`adjTable`).
  - `RULES` – the constructions from module-01 … module-12, each with a short explanation (`x`, listed in Ściąga → Gramatyka), the reason given for a mistake (`why`) and for a gloss (`gl`); `pair` lists words that are easily confused.
  - `analyse()` reads a sentence: each word's form and what governs it (preposition, verb, nie, number, noun, miło), the noun an adjective agrees with, and the verb's subject (pronoun, pan / pani, name, or a group joined by i).
  - `diagnose()` lines your answer up with the closest correct one and, per word, compares the tags and names every feature that differs (case, number, person, gender, infinitive), with the reason from the sentence. Different words that a rule pairs (jak / jaki / co, z / w, dobry / dobrze, być / mieć) get that rule. Words not in the list fall back to spelling: ogonki, rz / ż, rz / sz, ó / u, ch / h, ci / ć, -ii / -ji, stem changes (Kraków → Krakowie), a wrong ending, a different word.
  - `youSaid()` reads a wrong answer back in English, each word in its own form (z = from, mam = I have, one = they [no men]). When the question's English is known, only the changed words are swapped into it (“She lives from Warsaw.”); otherwise the word-by-word reading is shown (“I am as first name Jan.”). Same English as the correct answer → “Same meaning, only the ending.”; an unknown word one or two letters off → “Right word, one letter missing.” Unknown words and missing content words get no line rather than a guess.
  - The result sheet (`finish()`): the correct answer closest to yours with what you missed in green (whole words, or only the letters: ę for e), then *You said* and *Why*, `Dalej`, and the small actions (Napisz 3×, Była dobra, Za łatwe, Ukryj).
  - `glossAt()` builds every tap / hover gloss (meaning | form | why) from the same data; `gl(text, pre, post)` glosses a text in its sentence.
  - Content built from it: `VERBS`, `COUNTRIES`, `CITIES`, `NUMS`, the sentence-gap answers (`SENT`, from the subject) and oni / one (`ONI`, from the group). To add a word, add it to `LEX`; to add a rule, add it to `RULES` (and the preposition or verb that triggers it).
- **Learning engine** (`ENGINE`, one block between `ENGINE:BEGIN` and `ENGINE:END` in `app.html`): pure functions, no DOM or app globals, so `tests/engine.js` runs it on its own and the fit can run in a background worker.
  - `ENGINE.log` – the answer log. Every answer is an event `{id, t, term, ev, kind, res, sid, att, mode, ref, x}`: `ev` is `answer`, `seen` (intro card), `known` (“Znam to”), `easy` (“Za łatwe”), `amend` (“Była dobra”, pointing at the answer it corrects with `ref`) or `seed`; `kind` is `type`, `table`, `mc` or `reveal`; `sid` is the round, `att` the attempt number of the term in that round, `mode` the kind of round. Logs merge by union of event ids (so a sync never loses or doubles an answer); a reset is a time stamp (`resetAt`) that drops older events everywhere. On the wire, events are arrays, term ids are numbers into a term list and common values have one-letter codes; unknown values pass through, and a log from a newer version decodes to `null` so it is never overwritten. At most 15 000 events (the newest; seeds always stay).
  - `ENGINE.model` – the memory model: the chance of a right answer for a term at a given time. Forgetting follows the FSRS power law `R = (1 + 19/81 · t/S)^−0.5` (`R(S) = 90%`, half-life `12.8·S`). Per term, a stability `S` (days) and a difficulty `D` (its smoothed error rate). A right answer after a break makes `S` grow, the more the closer the term was to being forgotten and the easier it is; a right answer minutes after the last one hardly does; a miss cuts `S` (an ogonek slip less); a hint counts as a miss. Only answers after an hour or more away from the term count as evidence of forgetting (`MIN_GAP`).
  - Partial pooling: four global parameters (`s0`, `gain`, `lapse`, `slip`) are fitted to your answers as maximum likelihood × prior, so with few answers they stay at the defaults. Each family of terms (`FAMILIES`: verb forms, z + genitive, numbers, …; one line per new kind) has a forgetting multiplier with a prior centred on 1, so it only moves away from the global rate when its own answers show it, and a difficulty prior pooled from its error rate. Per-family rates are used only when, fitted on the older 80% of your answers, they predict the newest 20% better than one global rate (log loss); the fit report keeps that check and the calibration (predicted chance → how often you were right). Below 30 answers after a break the defaults are used; the comparison starts at 80.
  - `ENGINE.pick.thompson` – Thompson sampling: a plausible chance is drawn per term from a Beta around the model's chance, as wide as the term's evidence is thin; sorted lowest first.
  - `ENGINE.nut.step` – the Twardy orzech drill rule (below).
- **Services** around the engine: `AL` keeps the answer log on the device (`pl-test1-log`, device id `pl-device`), adds an event for every answer, intro card, “Za łatwe” and “Była dobra”, seeds the progress from before the log once (one `seed:<term>` event per term met: its level, mistakes, last result, and an assumed last practice half a day earlier), merges logs between tabs and in sync. `MEM` fits the model (in a background worker when the browser allows it, cached in `pl-test1-model` until 20 more answers or 6 hours) and gives each term's chance at a time, its evidence, and **readiness**: the average chance over all terms at the test (`EXAM`, a term not met counts 0), i.e. the expected score if you stopped now; 100% = expected to get any question right. Ustawienia → Model pamięci shows the fit, the per-family rates, the check on your newest answers and the calibration, and downloads the log.
- **Twardy orzech** (`planNut()`, `requeueAfter()`, `nutResults()`): a real round (first answers count, the round counter moves on; its score never cuts new terms elsewhere). Every met term gets its chance at the test; Thompson sampling draws one per term and the lowest draws fill the round (weak terms almost always, little-evidence terms often, solid terms rarely, a different mix each time). Terms practised in the last 20 minutes wait unless there are too few others. New terms come in when fewer met terms are under 95% than the round has places (up to the usual number of new terms). In the round, a miss comes back after 2 other questions; a right retry (or a term first met in this round) is checked once more after 6 others; right there = cracked. At most 4 attempts per term and 20 repeats per round. The results show cracked terms, readiness before → after, and what is not cracked yet.
- `tests/` – Playwright checks (need `playwright-core`; set `CHROME` to a Chromium binary). Serve the built folder first, e.g. `python3 -m http.server 8765`.
  - `rounds.js PORT` – plays ~14 rounds and checks the six round rules (no card for a written term, no warm-up for a written term, easy direction never counted, a term counts once per round, no same answer twice in a round, review only when due).
  - `diagnose.js PORT` – realistic wrong answers for every grammatical feature (on real terms and made-up sentences) and the explanation each must get, plus a sweep that makes typical slips on every term (case, verb person, ogonki) and checks each is named. Exits 1 on any failure; `VERBOSE=1` prints every explanation.
  - `yousaid.js PORT` – the examples from the design board (I live from Ghent, How do you call?, They [no men]…, Same meaning, only the ending…) and the exact line each must get, plus a sweep of automatic slips over every term (nothing may crash or print an empty line).
  - `grammar.js PORT` – the word list and rules are consistent (verb endings match their pattern, every noun has all its cases, accusative = genitive for people, generated adjectives), every word of every term is in the word list, and every Polish word on a teaching screen has a gloss.
  - `scoring.js PORT` – through the real screens: accent / hint slips never lower a level, multiple choice never lowers a level, a level drops only after 3 wrong written answers in a row.
  - `terms.js PORT` – every term id, the learning order and every term's prompts, accepted answers and options are unchanged against `terms-fixture.json` (record a new fixture with `node terms.js PORT write terms-fixture.json` only when a change is intended).
  - `engine.js` – the learning engine without a browser (`node engine.js`): log encode / merge / reset / seeds / newer versions, the forgetting curve and what each kind of answer does, a simulated learner with known forgetting rates (the fit points the right way, uses per-family rates only when they predict better, is calibrated, and beats the defaults on answers it has not seen), Thompson sampling, the drill rule, and speed.
  - `nut.js PORT` – Twardy orzech through the real screens: seeding, weighted random selection, drill to criterion (positions of the repeat and the check), the answer log, resuming mid-round, “Była dobra”, round counting, new terms, and the fit in a background worker.
  - `sync.js PORT` – two devices against a fake gist: both files created, logs merged both ways and identical, no rewrite when nothing is new, long files read from `raw_url`, a newer log left alone, and a reset reaching the other device.
  - `progress.js PORT` – a copy of saved progress (every term id, an open round) loads unchanged and the round resumes.
  - `feedback.js` / `feedback-round.js` (`PORT` env) – feedback button against a fake GitHub API.

Progress lives in localStorage `pl-test1` (term id → state; `_z` = time of the last reset, so a reset reaches the other devices), the open round in `pl-test1-round`, the answer log in `pl-test1-log`, the fitted memory model in `pl-test1-model` (a cache, rebuilt from the log), and is synced through a private gist (`pl-gh-token`, `pl-gh-gist`) as `progress.json` and `answers.json`. Term ids are the keys of that progress and of the log: keep them stable.

## Modes
Every exercise shows its mode above the question, by what you do, so later lessons slot in without new categories (`MODES`, `modeOf()` in `app.html`): **Rozmowa** (answer a Polish question), **Tłumaczenie** (English → Polish), **Znaczenie** (Polish → English), **Przekształcenie** (turn one sentence into another: formal / informal now; later tense, negation, plural), **Koniugacja** (a verb's form), **Deklinacja** (a noun's, adjective's or pronoun's form). Each has its colour (label, top of the card, answer box). Deklinacja shows the word being practised large on top. Koniugacja does not: verb forms are asked from the meaning (“we live” → mieszkamy, the person spelled out under it when English is ambiguous: ty / wy / pan / pani / oni / one), so finding the verb is part of the exercise. Verbs in sentences (`vs:`) are Tłumaczenie · całe zdanie: translate the whole English sentence; each term keeps its verb and person and draws one of several sentences (`SENT_TR`: a country, city, thing or first name changes), and a bracketed pronoun, czy and the place of teraz are optional.

**Hint ladder**: a question can carry `q.hints`, shown one per tap of Podpowiedź (1/3, 2/3 …); for verbs: the verb (mieszkać · to live), its family and endings, then the first letters of the form or the sentence with the verb left out. The answer event records how many were taken (`x.h`); the memory model reads a right answer after only the first hint as a near miss (like ogonki) and more help as a miss. Questions without `q.hints` keep the one old hint. Next to the mode only the unusual is shown: 2. próba, rozgrzewka · nie liczy się, nowe, dodatkowe, seria k/n. The terms' own eyebrow and sub text are unchanged (the terms snapshot still matches); the mode and the shorter line under the question are worked out when the question is shown.
